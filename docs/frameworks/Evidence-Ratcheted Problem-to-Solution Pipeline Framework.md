# RatchetAI: Evidence-Ratcheted Venture Engine
## Framework Reference v1.1

*The Evidence-Ratcheted Problem-to-Solution Venture Engine for Technopreneurship & Startup Incubators*  
*Empirical Validation Before Code. Relentless Evidence Over Polite Praise.*


---

## Core Principle

**Evidence determines advancement. Scores organize decisions. Founder enthusiasm never substitutes for evidence.**

Every phase demands stronger evidence than the previous phase. A later phase never inherits certainty just because the team likes an opportunity. The pipeline moves from landscape signals to validated problems, then to falsifiable solution hypotheses, and finally to empirical MVP testing.

### The Golden Rule of Discovery
> *Effective ideation searches for problems, each with a concrete, field-ready sufferer definition so you can go out and find people who are already bleeding cash and already spending to cope — no hypotheticals, no solution talk.*

Teams must avoid the **"What Can I Build" Fallacy** (starting from available tools or technical skills rather than broken systems). Ideation begins with empathy and deep problem interrogation, never with premature solution pitching.

## The Evidence Ratchet (Rule Table)

| What is ratcheted | Phase 1 (Discovery) | Phase 2 (Screening) | Phase 3 (Problem Validation) | Phase 4 (Solution Ideation) | Phase 5 (Solution Validation) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Research Mode** | Secondary (orient fast & cheap) | Secondary (triage & plausibility) | **Strictly Primary** (interviews & fieldwork for decisions) | Synthesis of validated Phase 3 facts | **Strictly Primary** (MVP experiments & behavioral tests) |
| **Evidence source** | Any credible source (PSA, news, forums, observation) | Existing evidence only | Firsthand interviews, direct observation, receipts/logs | From Phase 3 only — no new unvalidated problem claims | Real-world user interaction with MVP / prototype |
| **Evidence type minimum** | Single observation = Signal; two independent pieces = Documented | Behavioral plausibility (revealed sacrifice, not WTP) | Observed coping behavior + economic consequence | Assumes validated problem; focuses on causal mechanisms | Revealed behavioral commitment (pre-orders, deposits, signed LOIs, active use) |
| **Quantification required** | No | No | Yes (pesos, hours, units) | Inherited from Phase 3 | Yes (conversion %, task completion, savings achieved) |
| **Population scope** | No | Plausibility check only | Explicit estimation (bottom-up preferred) | Frozen from Phase 3 | Test cohort drawn from validated Phase 3 segment |
| **Verdicts allowed** | Signal / Documented / Strongly Documented | ADVANCE / SECOND LOOK / PARK | VALIDATED / REVALIDATE / REJECT | READY TO TEST / RE-IDEATE / RETURN TO PROBLEM | PURSUE / PIVOT / RETIRE CONCEPT |
| **Can move backward?** | n/a | Yes → re-screen after exit condition met | Yes → REVALIDATE closes gap | Yes → RETURN TO PROBLEM | Yes → PIVOT (to new mechanism) or RETURN TO PROBLEM |

## Phase 1 — Discovery

### Purpose
Breadth-first landscape research. Orient quickly and cheaply across Iloilo City and Province. Identify what problems exist, who experiences them, and what evidence suggests they are real. **Does not rank, shortlist, or determine startup potential.**

### Four Foundational Interrogation Questions
Before logging any problem, interrogate the problem space across four dimensions:
1. **Who experiences this problem?** Identify a specific target actor rather than a generic demographic.
2. **How painful is it, really?** Determine if friction is severe enough to warrant intervention.
3. **How often does it occur?** Establish recurrence cadence (high-frequency frictions vs. rare-but-catastrophic events).
4. **What do people currently do about it?** Identify existing informal workarounds, competitor tools, or habits.

### Three-Tier Evidence Classification

| Tier | Label | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| 🟡 | **Signal** | Single observation: one complaint, one anecdote, one news mention, one inference. Records the possibility; does not confirm the problem. |
| 🔵 | **Documented Problem** | Two or more reasonably independent pieces of evidence pointing to the same phenomenon. Clears the MEDIUM evidence confidence bar. Primary batch for Phase 2. |
| 🟢 | **Strongly Documented Problem** | Evidence from **multiple evidence types** — not just multiple sources of the same type (e.g., community reports + official statistics + academic research). Priority candidate for Phase 2. |

> **Field-research exception:** A Signal supported by strong direct behavioral evidence from fieldwork or personal observation may advance as `🔵 Documented — Primary Evidence Only` and must be flagged in Phase 2 as requiring further corroboration before Phase 3.

### Phase 1 Gate
Only 🔵 Documented and 🟢 Strongly Documented problems normally proceed to Phase 2. 🟡 Signals remain in the landscape for future corroboration.

### Evidence Source Hierarchy (Phase 1)

| Tier | Source type | Reliability | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Official statistics (PSA, DTI, DA, LGU data), peer-reviewed research | Highest | Macroscopic baseline & volume quantification |
| B | News reports, industry surveys, NGO/government program documentation | High | Trend validation & institutional friction |
| C | Community posts, firsthand accounts, group discussions | Medium | Ground-level sentiment & reported workarounds |
| D | Single anecdotes, inference, secondhand reports | Low | Signal capture only |

> **Crucial Rule:** Source tier ≠ evidence type quality. A Tier C post describing observed sacrifice behavior is stronger problem evidence than a Tier A paper reporting hypothetical preferences. Score both axes separately.

## Phase 2 — Screening & Shortlisting

### Purpose
Portfolio triage. From a batch of discovered problems, identify which earn real primary validation resources. **No primary field interviews required yet.**

### The 5 Screening Questions

| # | Screening Question | Criteria Name | What it measures | What to watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Real?** | **Pain Plausibility** | Does this read like something that genuinely hurts an actual person, or a hypothetical persona / nice-to-have? | Described frustration, named workaround, wasted time/money |
| 2 | **Frequent & Painful?** | **Frequency / Urgency Plausibility** | Does this recur often or matter urgently enough to force behavior change? | Recurring operational issue vs. rare one-off |
| 3 | **Big Enough?** | **Local Market Size Plausibility** | Is the addressable group in Iloilo large enough to matter, or is it an isolated preference? | Named population with a reference point — not "millions of Filipinos" |
| 4 | **Bleeding Cash?** | **Existing Sacrifice** | Is there observable evidence the target already pays, loses, or changes behavior because of this problem? | Revealed behavior: paying substitutes, losing inventory, wasted trips, duplicate work, hired extra help |
| 5 | **Reachable?** | **Access / Ability-to-Research** | Can the team realistically access, contact, and study the people who have this problem? | Named fieldwork path, existing contacts, plausible entry — scores access, not affinity |

> **Existing Sacrifice vs. WTP:** Existing sacrifice is revealed behavior (the person *already* pays or loses something). Willingness-to-pay is a stated future intention. These are not equivalent. Hypothetical WTP ("I'd pay for an app") is not a sacrifice signal.

> **Scores are patterns, not arithmetic.** Label every score `[Assumed]` or `[Demonstrated]`. The ADVANCE / SECOND LOOK / PARK verdict is read from the pattern of scores, not calculated from a sum.

### Winnability & Positional Advantage Advisory (Step 4B)
*In addition to objective problem screening, evaluate team-to-problem fit as an advisory note:*
* **Winnable?** Does the founding team possess — or can it readily acquire — an unfair advantage (domain knowledge, technical edge, unique distribution, regulatory access) in solving this specific problem?
* *Rule:* Winnability is an **execution advisory**, not a problem validity gate. An objective problem is real regardless of founder fit, but team selection prioritizes high-validity + high-winnability opportunities.

### Step 4A: Origin Pattern Tag (not scored)
Assign a tag for pattern recognition. Available tags: *Observed personal frustration / Existing workaround improved / Industry insider problem / Underserved customer segment / Coordination or marketplace failure / Cost reduction opportunity / Access problem / Information asymmetry / Changing external condition / Regulatory or institutional friction*.

### Verdict Definitions

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ✅ **ADVANCE** | Strong enough on all five criteria for real primary field investigation |
| ⚠️ **SECOND LOOK** | One specific criterion is weak; name the exact gap AND the mandatory exit condition (what evidence must be gathered and what threshold reached before re-screening) |
| ❌ **PARK** | Solution-in-disguise, vague sufferer, no local angle, or already occupied by a strong incumbent with no visible gap |

> **SECOND LOOK exit condition is mandatory.** A SECOND LOOK problem without a named exit condition is a parking lot with no exit.

## Phase 3 — Problem Validation

### Purpose
Primary evidence generation. Confirm through structured field investigation that the problem is real, recurring, costly, and experienced by a sizable identifiable segment. **Validates the problem — not the solution, not the business.**

### Primary Research & Interview Sequencing Protocol

```
Step 1: Problem Discovery Interviews (Phase 3)
   └── Present the PROBLEM only. Ask about past behavior & actual spending.
   └── NO pitching. NO solution descriptions. NO mockups.
   └── Objective: Confirm pain, frequency, workarounds, and economic sacrifice.

Step 2: Solution Concept Interviews (Phase 5 / MVP Stage)
   └── Only AFTER problem is VALIDATED.
   └── Show rough concept, prototype, or offer.
   └── Objective: Measure behavioral commitment (deposits, signed LOIs, active usage).
```

#### The "Mom Test" Anti-Pitching Defense
- **The "Polite Nod" Trap:** Pitching a solution idea during problem discovery yields false-positive encouragement (*"That sounds like a great app!"*). Polite nods are fatal to validation.
- **Behavioral Questions Only:** Ask *"Tell me about the last time you experienced [X]..."*, *"What did you do to solve it?"*, *"How much did that workaround cost you in pesos or hours?"*
- **The Rule of Revealed Action:** If the interviewee has not spent time, money, or effort trying to solve the problem in the last 6 months, the problem is not severe enough.

### 6-Level Validation Funnel (one level per turn, in order)

| Level | What it forces | Common failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Specific Sufferer** | Name the exact person: occupation + sector + Iloilo location | "Farmers" or "students" without specifics |
| **2. Demonstrated Pain** | Name the current workaround, tool, or habit they already use | "There's no solution" (not a workaround — that's a solution absence) |
| **3. Intensity & Frequency** (measured separately) | Frequency: how often? / Severity: how bad per occurrence? In concrete units | "Often" or "a lot" without numbers |
| **4. Local Market Size** | Estimate scope using bottom-up method or direct enumeration | Starting with "Philippine MSME = millions" |
| **5. Population / Market Evidence** | Population universe → target segment → reachable segment → validated sufferers | Conflating severity with scope |
| **6. Economic / Behavioral Consequence** | What does the **same segment from Level 5** already pay, lose, or sacrifice? | Consequence from a different segment, or jumping to "they'd pay for our app" |

> **Level 6 population-anchoring rule:** Economic consequence evidence must come from the same specific segment named and sized in Level 5. Evidence from a different segment or geography does not satisfy Level 6.

> **Level 3 separation rule:** A problem may be frequent-but-mild or rare-but-catastrophic. These are different problem types and must not be collapsed into a single "intensity" rating.

> **Mixed-answer handling (Levels 5–6):** If a student's response combines coping evidence with solution speculation, extract and acknowledge the coping evidence, redirect the solution speculation, and re-ask the level for the missing portion.

### Step 5: Two-Dimension Scorecard

| Dimension | Sub-criteria | Max score |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Evidence Confidence** | Direct user evidence; behavioral/workaround evidence; quantified consequence; recurrence; population evidence; source triangulation | **24** |
| **B. Problem Attractiveness** | Severity; frequency/urgency; existing sacrifice (Level 6); number affected; persistence despite workarounds | **20** |

> **Why two dimensions?** High confidence + low attractiveness = "well-documented trivial problem." High attractiveness + low confidence = "potentially serious but unconfirmed." Keep the dimensions separate.

### Verdict Definitions

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ✅ **VALIDATED** | Evidence Confidence is sufficiently high AND problem is sufficiently consequential. Earned the right to Phase 4. Does **not** mean "start building." |
| ⚠️ **REVALIDATE** | Problem may be real; one or more critical evidence gaps remain. Name the specific weak sub-criteria and what evidence would resolve them. |
| ❌ **REJECT** | Evidence contradicts the problem hypothesis, or demonstrated consequence is insufficient to justify further investment. |

## Phase 4 — Solution Ideation & Hypothesis Formation

### Purpose
Convert a validated problem into testable solution hypotheses structured on a **Simplified Validation Board (SVB)**. **Ends at experiment cards and validation boards, not a final product.**

### Step 2: Solution Brief (frozen from Phase 3 only)
Before any ideation: freeze the validated evidence. No new unvalidated assumptions. Missing fields marked `[not established in Phase 3]`.

### Step 3: Opportunity Question Formula
> *"How might we enable **[target actor]** to achieve **[desired outcome]** during **[situation / trigger]**, while respecting **[critical constraints]**?"*

### Step 4: Root-Mechanism Decomposition
Decompose the causal chain before generating concepts: *Information gap / Coordination failure / Timing mismatch / Access friction / Financial constraint / Behavioral pattern / Physical-infrastructure / Institutional-regulatory*.

### Step 5: Anti-Anchoring Rule (enforced minimum)
- **Minimum 5 concepts** before evaluation is permitted.
- **Minimum 3 mechanism families** (out of 15 standard families).
- **No shared delivery mechanism** (two apps with different features do not count as two mechanism families).
- *Technology is a delivery vehicle, not a mechanism.*

### Step 6: Concept Screening (6 criteria, scored 1–3 each, labeled [Hypothesis])
1. **Problem Fit** (Root mechanism vs. symptom)
2. **User Desirability Hypothesis** (Grounded in Phase 3 coping habits)
3. **Advantage over Status Quo** (Eliminates documented sacrifice)
4. **Feasibility Hypothesis** (Credible execution path)
5. **Viability Hypothesis** (Identifiable sustainable model)
6. **Evidence Testability** (Directly testable with cheap, fast methods)

### Step 7: Assumption Register (P1–P4 Priority Matrix)
Categorize assumptions across **Desirability**, **Feasibility**, **Behavioral**, **Value**, and **Viability**.
- **P1 (High Importance × High Uncertainty):** Must be tested first before any build commitment.

### Step 8: Experiment Cards & Simplified Validation Board (SVB)
For every P1 assumption, generate an Experiment Card:
`Assumption ➔ Falsifiable Hypothesis (If X then Y in Z% of cases) ➔ Test Method ➔ Target Participant ➔ Observable Metric ➔ Pass/Fail Threshold ➔ Decision if Pass / Decision if Fail`.

#### The Simplified Validation Board (SVB) Artifact Format
```
========================================================================================
SIMPLIFIED VALIDATION BOARD (SVB)
========================================================================================
[1] CUSTOMER SEGMENT:   [Target Actor + Specific Location from Phase 3]
[2] VALIDATED PROBLEM:  [Demonstrated Pain + Quantified Consequence from Phase 3]
[3] SOLUTION HYPOTHESIS: [Top ADVANCE Concept Mechanism + Delivery Vehicle from Phase 4]
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[4] CORE P1 ASSUMPTIONS:
    - Desirability: [Assumption text] (Priority: P1)
    - Behavioral:   [Assumption text] (Priority: P1)
    - Feasibility:  [Assumption text] (Priority: P1)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[5] EXPERIMENT & SUCCESS CRITERIA:
    - Test Method:     [Concierge / Wizard-of-Oz / Smoke Test / Prototype]
    - Observable Metric: [Concrete countable behavior]
    - Pass Threshold:  [e.g., ≥ 60% conversion or ≥ 15 pre-orders]
    - Fail Threshold:  [e.g., < 30% conversion]
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[6] DECISION RULE:
    - IF PASS ➔ Advance to Phase 5 MVP Pilot
    - IF FAIL ➔ Pivot (re-ideate mechanism) or Return to Phase 3 (re-validate problem)
========================================================================================
```

### Phase 4 Verdict Definitions

| Verdict | Trigger |
|---|---|
| 🟢 **READY TO TEST** | At least one ADVANCE concept has Experiment Cards and an SVB covering all P1 assumptions. Concrete test exists without full build. |
| 🟡 **RE-IDEATE** | Surviving concepts score low on Problem Fit / Testability, OR < 3 mechanism families represented. |
| 🔴 **RETURN TO PROBLEM** | Decomposition contradicts Phase 3 evidence, or no concept resolves the core problem. |

## Phase 5 — Solution Validation & MVP Experimentation

### Purpose
Execute minimal viable experiments to test P1 hypotheses in the real world. Measure **revealed behavioral commitment** (not polite opinions) to decide whether to Pursue, Pivot, or Retire the concept.

### The Lean Startup Experimentation Loop (Build-Measure-Learn)

```
       ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
       │   PHASE 4: Solution Hypothesis & SVB   │
       └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                           │
                           ▼
                  ┌─────────────────┐
                  │ 1. MINIMUM TEST │ (Fastest, cheapest test of P1 assumption)
                  └────────┬────────┘
                           │
                           ▼
                  ┌─────────────────┐
                  │   2. MEASURE    │ (Count actual behaviors: cash, time, signups)
                  └────────┬────────┘
                           │
                           ▼
                  ┌─────────────────┐
                  │    3. LEARN     │ (Compare against pre-set Pass/Fail threshold)
                  └────────┬────────┘
                           │
          ┌────────────────┼────────────────┐
          ▼                ▼                ▼
   🟢 PURSUE / BUILD   🟡 PIVOT       🔴 RETIRE
   (Exceeded Pass)     (Failed Mech)   (Problem Unsolvable)
```

### Minimum Viable Experiment Archetypes

| Test Archetype | What it tests | How it works without building full software |
|---|---|---|
| **Concierge MVP** | Value & Desirability | Perform the service manually behind the scenes directly for the customer. |
| **Wizard of Oz** | Desirability & Usability | Front-end appears automated; back-end is operated manually by the founders. |
| **Smoke / Landing Page Test** | Demand & Acquisition | Simple landing page or brochure detailing the value proposition with a "Pre-order" or "Request Access" call to action. |
| **Paper / Clickable Mockup** | Workflow & Comprehension | Interactive Figma or paper wireframe tested in-person with target actors. |
| **Letter of Intent (LOI) / Deposit** | Economic Commitment | Formal non-binding agreement or refundable cash deposit securing early access. |

### The Behavioral Commitment Hierarchy (Phase 5 Evidence)

From strongest to weakest evidence of solution validation:
1. **Financial Commitment:** Pre-orders, upfront cash deposits, signed purchase contracts, paid pilots.
2. **Behavioral Sacrifice:** Replacing an existing daily tool, investing hours inputting data, rearranging workflow.
3. **Reputational / Social Commitment:** Introducing the founder to their boss/peers, public endorsement.
4. **Contact / Time Commitment:** Providing private phone number, attending multiple scheduled sessions.
5. **Polite Interest (Zero Evidence):** *"Looks great,"* *"Let me know when it launches,"* *"I would definitely buy that."*

### Phase 5 Verdict Definitions

| Verdict | Meaning | Next Step |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 **PURSUE** | Experiment met or exceeded the Pass Threshold on all P1 assumptions. Strong revealed behavioral commitment documented. | Proceed to functional MVP development and early adopter cohort rollout. |
| 🟡 **PIVOT** | Failed Pass Threshold on a solution or behavioral assumption, but problem remains validated. | Return to Phase 4; select an alternative mechanism family on the Validation Board. |
| 🔴 **RETIRE CONCEPT** | Multiple mechanism pivots failed, or customers repeatedly refuse to alter existing workarounds. | Return to Phase 1/2 with learnings documented in the research archive. |

## Evidence Type Hierarchy (Cross-Phase)

From strongest to weakest as validation evidence:

1. **Observed behavior / transaction** — the researcher directly saw or recorded the person execute an action or payment.
2. **Remembered behavior** — the person recounts what they actually did in a specific past instance.
3. **Specific reported behavior** — the person names dates, tools, and concrete amounts spent.
4. **General opinion** — the person describes their situation in broad, abstract terms.
5. **Hypothetical intention** — *"I would pay for…"* or *"I would use…"* (treat as unverified assumption).
6. **Founder assumption** — the team believes someone has this problem without verification.

## Persistent Rules (All Phases)

1. **Evidence determines advancement.** Scores organize decisions. Enthusiasm does not substitute.
2. **Every phase demands stronger evidence than the previous.** No phase inherits the certainty of a prior phase.
3. **Secondary research orients (Phases 1–2); Primary research decides (Phases 3 & 5).**
4. **Existing sacrifice > stated WTP.** Revealed coping behavior is infinitely stronger evidence than purchase intention statements.
5. **Problem Interviews first, Solution Interviews second.** Never pitch solutions during problem discovery.
6. **Scores are patterns, not arithmetic.** Do not aggregate multi-criteria scores into a single composite number at Phase 2.
7. **Two scorecard dimensions stay separate at Phase 3.** Evidence Confidence and Problem Attractiveness answer different questions.
8. **Founder affinity is an execution advisory, not a validity gate.** A problem is real regardless of who discovered it.
9. **SECOND LOOK requires an exit condition.** A SECOND LOOK verdict without a named exit condition has no path to resolution.
10. **VALIDATED ≠ "build now."** It means the problem has earned the right to Phase 4 ideation.
11. **No concept is VALIDATED until Phase 5.** Phase 4's strongest outcome is READY TO TEST on a Simplified Validation Board.
12. **Technology is a delivery vehicle, not a solution mechanism.** Mechanism families describe what a concept does, not how it is coded.
13. **Backward movement is a research success, not a failure.** The ratchet enforces forward progress only through evidence.

---

## Anti-Fabrication Rules (Applies to All Phases)

When generating or evaluating local reference points:
- Do not invent Iloilo City barangay or municipality names — use only named places that actually exist in the Iloilo context.
- Do not silently convert national statistics to local figures without disclosing the assumption.
- Do not fabricate PSA, DTI, or DA figures — label any estimate `[estimated — verify against official source]`.
- If no local figure exists, state so explicitly rather than approximating from a national figure without disclosure.
